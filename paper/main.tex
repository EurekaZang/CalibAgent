\begin{abstract}
Commercial quadrupeds expose desired planar velocity commands while hiding the
controller and dynamics that determine realized motion. Cross-axis coupling,
asymmetric response, and deployment shifts can therefore preserve balance while
corrupting the variable used by a navigation planner. We present \method\
(Goal-Aware Uncertainty-Guided \mbox{Exploration}), a task-aware active calibration and
shift-recovery layer for opaque quadruped velocity interfaces. \method\ learns a
coupled Bayesian response model and allocates hard-authorized trials according
to a planner-facing task distribution. The same posterior supports validated
stopping, bounded inverse compensation, and residual-triggered active recovery.
On 183 passive Unitree Go2 trials, coupled affine calibration reduces held-out
root-mean-square error (RMSE) by 54.5\% relative to raw commands. Across
controlled synthetic families, task weighting reaches a joint
accuracy--uncertainty target in 18.67 trials, saving 39.5\% over Latin hypercube
sampling and 18.8\% over D-optimal design; a no-task ablation and a
task-distribution negative control localize the benefit to the declared task
measure. Fixed-configuration Isaac Lab experiments yield 9.3--31.7\% held-out RMSE
reductions. Across four shifts, detection reaches 99.3--100\%, recovery reaches
97.9--100\%, and task-weighted selection improves early recovery over passive
updating. Across six held-out Isaac Sim maps, failure-aware navigation is
noninferior to a dense reference. A fixed-planner Go2 comparison further shows more continuous
qualitative progress under command compensation. Together, these results show
how an opaque velocity interface can become a task-conditioned calibrated
control variable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A quadruped can remain upright while executing the wrong motion. High-level
controllers accept a desired planar velocity
$\vect{u}=[v_x,v_y,\omega_z]^\top$, but the steady body velocity may contain
gain error, dead zones, saturation, and cross-axis coupling. These errors are
particularly consequential when a planner assumes that the exposed command is
a calibrated control variable. With a limited interaction budget, calibration
must decide which executable commands to measure when deployment occupies only
a structured subset of the admissible command space.

Three lines of prior work expose the missing connection. Taouil \emph{et al.}
learned center-of-mass and footstep response models from exhaustive command
permutations of a black-box quadruped controller and integrated them into
footstep planning \cite{taouil2023blackbox}. Carrillo \emph{et al.} selected
calibration configurations by downstream tool-center-point error and used that
task-space quantity to assess when calibration was sufficient
\cite{carrillo2013task}. SPI-Active optimizes legged-robot excitation using
Fisher information, but identifies simulator-facing physical parameters for
sim-to-real transfer \cite{sobanbabu2025spiactive}. This leaves a distinct
calibration problem: directing a limited trial budget toward the external
command interface itself when neither the low-level controller nor its physical
model is available.

Legged adaptation usually changes the controller or estimates variables used
inside it. Rapid Motor Adaptation conditions a learned policy on latent
environment estimates \cite{kumar2021rma}; safe Bayesian optimization can tune
feedback gains directly \cite{widmer2023safe}; and online system identification
can estimate mass and center of mass before updating a model-based controller
\cite{haack2025adaptive}. Residual and embodiment adaptation likewise operate on
controller or policy state \cite{sun2021unknown,fey2024adapt,li2026embodiment}.
\method\ instead calibrates the user-visible command-to-motion interface while
leaving the hidden dynamics and controller unchanged.

\method\ treats calibration as a deployment lifecycle (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
A planner-facing task measure specifies which desired velocities the planner is
likely to request. The
response posterior then selects informative commands, quantifies whether
validation is sufficient, and supplies a bounded inverse from desired motion to
the opaque interface. A non-learned authorization layer remains the sole arbiter
of execution. Frozen residual signatures detect when the calibrated map has
changed; a latched alarm reopens task-weighted calibration and returns to
validation before normal operation resumes.

The contributions are threefold. First, we formulate opaque velocity-interface
calibration as deployment-task-conditioned Bayesian experimental design, with
planner-facing predictive uncertainty determining trial selection. Second, we
use one response posterior throughout an operational lifecycle that separates
information ranking from hard authorization and supports validated stopping,
bounded inversion, and residual-triggered active recovery. Third, we establish
an evidence ladder: passive Go2 data test model need; controlled synthetic and
Isaac experiments isolate acquisition and recovery mechanisms; held-out
navigation tests downstream consequence; and a fixed-planner physical
comparison provides qualitative interface-level evidence.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Black-Box Command Models}

Velocity-controlled robots can be calibrated from externally measured motion
without access to their internal dynamics. Li and St\"uckler jointly calibrate a
velocity-control kinematic model within visual--inertial odometry
\cite{li2022vio}. For quadrupeds, Taouil \emph{et al.} learn motion models of an
opaque controller and use them in an A*-based footstep planner
\cite{taouil2023blackbox}. Their calibration data exhaust the command
permutations of interest. \method\ treats data collection itself as the limited
resource and allocates it according to the command distribution induced by the
downstream planner.

\subsection{Active and Task-Oriented Calibration}

Robot calibration has long treated measurement configuration as a design
variable \cite{hollerbach1996calibration,calafiore2001dynamic}.
Observability and alphabetic criteria target parameter identification
\cite{sun2008observability,sun2008active}, whereas task-oriented calibration
scores configurations by uncertainty in an operational quantity
\cite{carrillo2013task}. Goal-oriented Bayesian design formalizes the same shift
from global parameter uncertainty to a quantity of interest
\cite{attia2018goal}. SPI-Active brings active excitation to legged system
identification by maximizing trajectory Fisher information for physical
parameters \cite{sobanbabu2025spiactive}. \method\ applies task-directed design
to a different estimand: the external velocity response under a
planner-facing command measure.

\subsection{Legged Adaptation and Safety-Filtered Exploration}

Policy adaptation handles changing embodiment or terrain through latent state,
residual models, or short interaction histories
\cite{kumar2021rma,sun2021unknown,fey2024adapt,li2026embodiment}. Widmer
\emph{et al.} instead optimize locomotion-controller gains with contextual safe
Bayesian optimization \cite{widmer2023safe}, while Haack \emph{et al.} estimate
mass and horizontal center of mass and feed reliable estimates back into a
model-based controller \cite{haack2025adaptive}. These methods adapt internal
policy, controller, or physical-model variables. \method\ operates at the
external command boundary. Its acquisition is hard-authorized: a non-learned
filter checks every ranked proposal before execution, without asserting a
probabilistic safety guarantee.

\section{GAUGE}

\subsection{Problem Setting and Task Measure}

The low-level controller is fixed and opaque. A trial applies a constant
body-frame command $\vect{u}\in\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and extracts a
robust steady-motion observation $\vect{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ with measurement
covariance $R$. Invalid observations are rejected before the dataset
$\mathcal{D}_n=\{(\vect{u}_i,\vect{y}_i,R_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ is updated. A finite
design pool $\mathcal{C}$ defines the modeled command envelope; runtime state
$\vect{x}$ induces a separate hard-authorized subset
$\mathcal{A}(\vect{x})\subseteq\mathcal{C}$.

The deployment task is a weighted command measure
$\mathcal{T}=\{(\vect{g}_j,w_j)\}_{j=1}^{G}$ with $\sum_jw_j=1$. Nominal
waypoint-planner outputs provide the support points $\vect{g}_j$. Given response
posterior covariance $\vect{\Sigma}_a$ for output axis $a$, the task-facing
epistemic uncertainty is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_n)=
 \sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{G} w_j
 \vect{\phi}(\vect{g}_j)^\top\vect{\Sigma}_a
 \vect{\phi}(\vect{g}_j).
 \label{eq:taskvariance}
\end{equation}
Calibration aims to reduce this deployment-facing quantity under the available
trial budget, rather than reconstructing the response uniformly over
$\mathcal{C}$.

\subsection{Bayesian Command-to-Motion Model}

For command $\vect{u}$ and measured steady velocity $\vect{y}\in\mathbb{R}^3$,
the coupled affine basis is $[1,v_x,v_y,\omega_z]$; a diagonal control zeros
off-axis coefficients. The nonlinear 13-term basis adds pairwise products and
$[v_k-\tau_k]_+,[-v_k-\tau_k]_+$, with
$\vect{\tau}=[0.15,0.10,0.25]$. Non-intercept columns are standardized once on
the design pool, and the closed-loop prior regularizes the linear projection
toward identity.

For output axis $a$, the model is
\begin{equation}
 y_a=\vect{\theta}_a^\top\vect{\phi}(\vect{u})+\epsilon_a,
 \quad
 \epsilon_a\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_a^2+r_a),
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $r_a$ is the measurement-pipeline covariance diagonal and $\sigma_a^2$
is the frozen process variance. Given prior
$\mathcal{N}(\vect{m}_{a0},\vect{\Sigma}_{a0})$, sequential updates use
\begin{align}
 \vect{\Lambda}_a &\leftarrow \vect{\Lambda}_a+
 \frac{\vect{\phi}\vect{\phi}^\top}{\sigma_a^2+r_a}, \\
 \vect{\eta}_a &\leftarrow \vect{\eta}_a+
 \frac{\vect{\phi}y_a}{\sigma_a^2+r_a},
 \quad
 \vect{\Sigma}_a=\vect{\Lambda}_a^{-1},\quad
 \vect{m}_a=\vect{\Sigma}_a\vect{\eta}_a .
 \label{eq:update}
\end{align}
The predictive mean is
$\mu_a(\vect{u})=\vect{m}_a^\top\vect{\phi}(\vect{u})$. The response variance
used by bounded inversion is
$s_a^2(\vect{u})=\vect{\phi}(\vect{u})^\top\vect{\Sigma}_a
\vect{\phi}(\vect{u})+\sigma_a^2$; coverage of a future measured response also
adds its observation variance $r_a$. Acquisition uses only the reducible
epistemic term. Coupled input features represent cross-axis response while the
three output posteriors remain conditionally independent.

\subsection{Task-Weighted Active Calibration}

Uniformly reducing uncertainty over the admissible envelope wastes trials when
deployment concentrates on a structured command distribution. For candidate
$\vect{c}\in\mathcal{C}$, task-weighted integrated variance reduction (IVR),
obtained by a Sherman--Morrison update, gives the conditional one-step reduction
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taskvariance}:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{I}(\vect{c})=
 \sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{G} w_j
 \frac{\left(\vect{\phi}(\vect{g}_j)^\top\vect{\Sigma}_a
 \vect{\phi}(\vect{c})\right)^2}
 {\sigma_a^2+\tilde r_a(\vect{c})+
 \vect{\phi}(\vect{c})^\top\vect{\Sigma}_a
 \vect{\phi}(\vect{c})}.
 \label{eq:ivr}
\end{equation}
The optional $\tilde r_a(\vect{c})$ predicts candidate observation-noise
variance; the posterior update always uses the realized $r_a$. The selector maximizes
$\mathcal{I}(\vect{c})-\lambda_mP_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vect{c})-
\lambda_{\Delta}P_{\mathrm{move}}(\vect{c})$, where the two penalties limit
command magnitude and travel. Greedy batches use
outcome-independent covariance updates. This score ranks information value;
the hard authorization layer makes the execution decision.

\subsection{Authorization, Stopping, and Bounded Inversion}

The hard filter checks state validity, localization, battery, attitude, base
height, command and slew bounds, combined normalized linear--angular load, and
workspace occupancy. It
fails closed if no ranked candidate remains, and an independent 50~Hz monitor
can zero commands. Statistical value cannot relax an execution limit.

Stopping requires minimum coverage and three consecutive checks of held-out
validation RMSE and task-facing uncertainty; hard budgets take priority. During
deployment, the inverse searches the bounded candidate set for planner target
$\vect{v}^{*}$ and minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \|W(\vect{\mu}(\vect{u})-\vect{v}^{*})\|_2^2+\lambda
 \|W(\vect{u}-\vect{v}^{*})\|_2^2+\rho\sum_a s_a^2(\vect{u}),
 \label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
where $W$ normalizes the three axes and $\lambda,\rho\geq0$ trade command
regularization against predictive risk. The selected command then passes
through the hard filter.

\subsection{Shift Detection and Active Recovery}

Shift monitoring cycles through four authorized commands
$(-.20,0,-.20)$, $(-.20,0,.20)$, $(.12,-.10,-.15)$, and
$(.12,.10,.15)$. Commissioning freezes the corresponding residual signatures
$\vect{r}^{0}_{j}=\vect{y}_{j}-\vect{\mu}(\vect{u}_{j})$. For a later response
to signature command $j(t)$, the current residual is
$\vect{r}_t=\vect{y}_t-\vect{\mu}(\vect{u}_{j(t)})$, and the detector evaluates
\begin{equation}
 d_t=\left\|\operatorname{diag}(.14,.08,.18)^{-1}
 \left(\vect{r}_t-\vect{r}^{0}_{j(t)}\right)\right\|_2 .
 \label{eq:signature}
\end{equation}
An alarm requires $d_t>.30$ on at least two of the last four valid monitor
trials and a two-trial minimum dwell. The alarm latches, inflates epistemic
covariance by a factor of eight without changing the posterior mean, and opens
a 12-command task-weighted recovery budget. Normal validation stopping resumes
after recovery.

\section{Experiments and Results}

We organize the evaluation around four questions. \textbf{Q1:} Is coupled
calibration necessary for a real black-box quadruped velocity interface?
\textbf{Q2:} Does task weighting reduce calibration effort relative to passive,
parameter-oriented, and task-agnostic designs? \textbf{Q3:} Can the calibrated
interface detect deployment shifts and recover without conflating information
ranking with execution authorization? \textbf{Q4:} Do command-level gains
translate into navigation? Hardware evidence comprises passive model data and a
qualitative fixed-planner comparison; active-selection and recovery mechanisms
are tested in controlled synthetic and fixed-configuration Isaac experiments.

\subsection{Coupled Response Modeling Is Necessary}

The passive Go2 data show that an intercept alone cannot absorb off-axis
response (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}a). The dataset contains 183 valid trials in
three 61-trial sessions, measured with a Livox MID-360 running
FAST-LIO~\cite{xu2021fastlio}. Leave-one-session-out folds compare raw commands with diagonal and
coupled linear/affine maps. Pooled RMSE is 0.06605 for raw commands, 0.04828 for
diagonal linear, 0.04563 for diagonal affine, 0.03517 for coupled linear, and
0.03009 for coupled affine. Coupled linear therefore improves on diagonal
affine without adding an intercept. The coupled-affine map reduces error by
54.45\% relative to raw commands and 34.07\% relative to diagonal affine; its
three held-out-session RMSE values are 0.02888, 0.03158, and 0.02974. Lateral
RMSE falls from 0.08459 to 0.02569, while the yaw-to-lateral coefficient remains
0.119--0.129 across holdouts. A 30-trial fit is within 3.33\% of the 122-trial
dense fit.

Controlled closed-loop runs test whether calibration improves realized motion
under physics. Four Isaac Lab contexts \cite{mittal2025isaaclab} use 20 paired
seeds, 12 calibration commands, and eight held-out commands with a common robust
constant-twist estimator. Raw-to-calibrated RMSE is
0.15199$\rightarrow$0.10635 for affine distortion,
0.15054$\rightarrow$0.10282 for dead-zone distortion,
0.10741$\rightarrow$0.09742 for low friction with load and center-of-mass
offset, and 0.09554$\rightarrow$0.07849 on rough terrain with load and offset
(Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}b). Relative reductions span 9.30--31.70\%, and all
20 paired seeds improve in every context. The hardware inference is passive
model need; the paired Isaac runs supply the closed-loop intervention test.

\subsection{Task Weighting Reduces Calibration Effort}

The acquisition study uses 20 paired seeds in affine, dead-zone, and
heteroscedastic synthetic families. All methods share a six-command seed and a
160-trial horizon over
$[-1,1]\times[-.5,.5]\times[-1.5,1.5]$, with
$\|(v_x,v_y)\|_2\leq1$. Frozen draws vary gain, off-axis coupling, bias,
saturation, and noise. The declared task measure weights forward, left-turn,
and right-turn command components by $(.50,.25,.25)$. The stopping target is
RMSE $\leq0.04$ and integrated epistemic variance $\leq0.0015$. Random, Latin
hypercube sampling (LHS), Sobol, D-optimal, no-task IVR, and a dense oracle form
the controls; acquisition penalties are zero. A hidden 400-command grid defines
crossing and a disjoint 1,024-command grid audits it. Paired Wilcoxon contrasts
use Holm correction, and paired bootstrap intervals use 4,000--5,000
resamples.

Task IVR reaches the joint target in 18.67 trials, compared with 30.87 for LHS,
30.67 for random, 25.72 for Sobol, 23.00 for D-optimal, and 25.67 for no-task IVR
(Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}c). The corresponding savings are 39.52\%, 39.13\%,
27.41\%, 18.84\%, and 27.27\%; all paired contrasts remain significant after
Holm correction. The primary LHS contrast saves 12.20 trials [10.73, 13.73].
The no-task comparison identifies the planner-facing measure, beyond generic
active selection, as the source of the gain.

Realized held-out error preserves this ordering (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}d).
At budgets 18, 24, and 30, task IVR RMSE is 0.02143, 0.01684, and 0.01420. Its
paired improvement is 0.00392--0.00466 over D-optimal and 0.00545--0.00881 over
no-task IVR; all six 95\% intervals exclude zero. The uncertainty gate binds in
all 60 active conditions, with family-level crossings at 18, 18, and 20 trials.
The disjoint audit yields crossing RMSE 0.01796--0.02315 and axiswise predictive
coverage 0.917--0.955. Supplying exact heteroscedastic candidate noise moves one
crossing from 20 to 21 trials and preserves the 11-trial lead over the two
principal controls.

The benefit follows the declared deployment support. Frozen acquisitions retain
positive paired differences relative to task IVR under forward- and right-heavy
navigation mixtures, and the left-heavy D-optimal contrast becomes positive by
30 trials (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}e). When evaluation expands to the uniform
authorized envelope, the ordering reverses at 24 trials and task IVR error becomes
3.21 times its error under the declared measure. Task-directed calibration thus
trades global coverage for lower error on the planner-facing distribution.

\subsection{Operational Robustness and Shift Recovery}

Replay tests first verify the separation between ranking and authorization.
Across 60 acquisition traces, the hard filter rejects 300/300 hazardous
proposals, rejects 0/20 authorized controls, and catches all 160 injected state faults.
The stopping rule makes no premature decision on 60 trajectories; median and
p95 overshoot are two trials. The 50~Hz physics monitor has a maximum 20~ms abort
interval. These tests establish the implemented authorization and stopping
behavior under replay and simulation, not a physical safety guarantee.

Four held-out shifts are evaluated: combined friction, load, and gain; gain and
coupling; physical and nonlinear response; and load, center of mass, and gain
(Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}a). Each shift pools two disjoint 72-seed blocks.
Fixed-model, passive-update, and \method\ arms share 12 initial commands, four
residual signatures, five monitor commands, a 12-command recovery budget, and
rolling validation. The pre-specified early endpoint averages recovery trials
4--9. Stationary monitoring covers 120 sequences and 12,000 valid trials.

The stationary monitor produces 0/120 sequence alarms, with a two-sided exact
95\% upper bound of 0.0303. Detection is 143/144 for two shifts and 144/144 for
the other two; the lowest exact interval is [0.9619, 0.9998]. \method\ recovers
in 141/144 friction/load/gain trials and 144/144 elsewhere, with lowest interval
[0.9403, 0.9957]. Worst p95 detection and recovery delays are 3.9 and 9 trials,
equivalent to 10.1~s of monitor commands and 46.8~s for the fixed
recovery-plus-validation schedule.

Active recovery lowers early RMSE relative to passive updating by 0.00936,
0.01053, 0.01847, and 0.01192 across the four shifts; every paired 95\%
interval is positive (Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}b). Terminal RMSE ranges from
0.09762 to 0.11227, and every interval upper endpoint remains below 0.116 and
the declared 0.14 gate (Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}c). The selector ablation keeps
detection, updating, and recovery budgets fixed. Task IVR lowers early RMSE by
0.02192 versus D-optimal, 0.01583 versus no-task IVR, 0.00937 versus LHS, and
0.00986 versus random selection, with positive paired intervals throughout
(Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}d). The early-recovery gain therefore depends on the
task-directed selector rather than detection and posterior updating alone.

\subsection{Navigation Consequence}

Held-out navigation crosses six disjoint maps, seven calibration methods, and
72 paired seeds per map, for 3,024 episodes (Fig.~\ref{fig:navigation}). All arms
share the controller, 50~Hz interlock, 10~Hz waypoint follower, geometry, and
randomization. \method\ and matched controls use 12 calibration commands; the
dense reference uses 30, followed by eight common validation commands. Task IVR
ranks a 512-command design pool under the planner-facing task measure. Routes
span 2.85--3.60~m, with a 0.79~m minimum opening and 0.20~m collision radius.
Success requires reaching the 0.25~m goal ball without collision or simulator
termination (a serious event) by 60~s, and every failure retains the 60-s cap.
The declared
noninferiority margins are a 1.25 capped-time ratio and a $-0.05$ success
difference.

\method\ succeeds in 72/72 episodes on five maps and 70/72 on weighted arc;
raw commands succeed in 0/72 or 1/72 (Fig.~\ref{fig:navigation}a). Every
\method\ collision count is zero. The 95\% lower endpoints of the paired
raw-minus-\method\ capped-time gain are 23.45--32.68~s
(Fig.~\ref{fig:navigation}b). At the same 12-trial budget, task IVR reduces
validation RMSE over D-optimal by 9.98--12.28\% on every map; its advantage over
no-task IVR is positive on five maps and unresolved on S-bend
(Fig.~\ref{fig:navigation}c).

The worst \method/dense capped-time ratio upper bound is 1.0736, below the 1.25
margin, and every paired success-difference lower bound exceeds $-0.05$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:navigation}d). The two weighted-arc timeouts are neither
collisions nor simulator terminations. Acquisition takes 31.2~s for \method\ and 78.0~s
for dense calibration, or 52.0 and 98.8~s after common validation. Across its
432 episodes, \method\ records 10 authorization interventions and no simulator
termination.

A qualitative physical comparison uses the same fixed DRL-DCLP local planner
\cite{zhang2025drldclp} with either direct commands or the frozen \method\
inverse. One complete run per condition is recorded from a fixed camera in the
same obstacle layout. At matched wall-clock times, the direct run accumulates

interface-level behavioral comparison; one run per condition does not estimate
a trajectory-level effect or run-to-run variability.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Task weighting converts deployment structure into calibration efficiency. When
the planner concentrates commands on a structured subset of the interface,
global identification spends trials on responses that do not affect the task.
The no-task ablation shows that sequential information gain alone does not
achieve the same trial efficiency, and the broad-uniform negative control gives the complementary
boundary: once deployment support expands, the advantage disappears and can
reverse. The task measure is therefore part of the calibration specification,
not a fixed property of the robot.

Each evidence tier answers a different part of the argument. Passive Go2 data
establish the need for a coupled external response model. Synthetic controls
isolate the acquisition principle, and fixed-configuration Isaac experiments test
closed-loop calibration, authorization logic, shift detection, and active
recovery. Held-out simulator maps connect command calibration to quantitative
navigation outcomes. The fixed-planner Go2 comparison adds qualitative
behavior-level evidence without replacing a replicated physical endpoint.

The current model targets a steady command--response map. Strong history
dependence, rapid transients, or gait and controller switching require an
expanded response state or separate models. A changed planner distribution
requires a new task measure, as the support-expansion control demonstrates.
Quantitative physical evaluation of online active acquisition, structured
shifts, and recovery remains the main step needed to test whether the complete
lifecycle transfers beyond the fixed simulator configuration.

\section{Conclusion}

\method\ turns an opaque quadruped velocity interface into a task-conditioned
calibrated control variable. It directs a limited trial budget toward
planner-facing uncertainty, validates the learned response before bounded
compensation, and reopens active calibration when residual signatures indicate
a shift. Passive Go2 data establish coupled model need; controlled synthetic
and Isaac experiments establish task-weighted acquisition and recovery; and
held-out navigation establishes downstream consequence.
